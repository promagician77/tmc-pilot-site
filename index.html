A working model of the 6-week pilot: who to verify, who to mail first, how fast to ramp without tripping Gmail’s spam limits, and what Amazon will and won’t report back. It runs on a synthetic list with stated assumptions until the real export arrives; swap the file and every number below recalculates.

What the model found

    Who gets mailed

    Verification first, then tiers by the last time someone clicked, bought or visited the site. Opens are left out on purpose: Apple Mail opens every message automatically, so an open says nothing about a person.

    How fast to send

    Gmail judges the sending domain on its spam rate across all mail and wants it under 0.3%, ideally under 0.1%. The unknown that decides the pilot is how often cold contacts hit “report spam”. The ramp finds out on the first 5,000 to 15,000 of them instead of on all 50,000.

    Pre-flight: authentication check

    Runs live in your browser through Cloudflare’s public DNS-over-HTTPS service. Nothing is stored.

      Simulated pilot, 24 Nov to 4 Jan

      One send to each tier at most every 2 days, because Gmail’s figure arrives late. Medium starts after warm reads green, cold after medium. Each batch is sized so the projected 7-day rate stays under 0.2%, and under 0.3% allowing for ordinary complaint noise. Cold is planned at 0.5% until its own numbers arrive.

        What fires the flows

        Klaviyo flows react to events it receives about a person. The brand’s own store and the landing page can send those events. Amazon can’t: it reports clicks, detail page views, add-to-carts and purchases per Attribution tag, never per shopper.

        What the money can tell you

        Meta can’t see Amazon purchases, so the ads optimise for the landing page’s email sign-up and Amazon sales are counted through Attribution tags. The real question for a $1,500 to $2,500 test is which decisions it has enough data to make.

            Not measured

            • The real list. Every count here comes from a synthetic 200,000-row list with the assumptions shown above.
            • Complaint, bounce and click rates per tier. They are inputs; the first batches replace them.
            • Live DNS for themanechoice.com from the build machine (its network blocks DNS). The check above runs in your browser instead.
            • Calls to Klaviyo, Amazon or Meta. Requests are checked against Klaviyo’s spec, not sent. The spec doesn’t forbid unknown extra fields, so a misspelt optional field would pass.
            • NeverBounce pricing: published figures disagree, so both ends are shown.
